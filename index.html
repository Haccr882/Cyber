Encryption converts readable information into a protected representation using an algorithm and a cryptographic key. It is a foundational technique for protecting information.

7. Symmetric vs Asymmetric Encryption

Symmetric and asymmetric cryptography solve different problems. Symmetric systems use a shared secret, while asymmetric systems use related public and private keys.

8. What Is a Firewall?

A firewall is a security control that permits or blocks network traffic according to rules. Firewalls can exist on individual devices, servers, networks, and cloud environments.

9. What Is Malware?

Malware is a general term for software or code intended to perform unwanted or harmful actions. Common categories include ransomware, spyware, worms, and trojans.

10. Virus vs Worm vs Trojan

Virus, worm, and trojan describe different malware characteristics. Knowing the distinctions helps learners understand how malicious software can spread or disguise itself.

Phishing is a form of social engineering that attempts to trick people into revealing information, opening harmful content, or visiting deceptive websites.

12. Social Engineering Explained

Social engineering manipulates human behavior rather than relying exclusively on a technical vulnerability. Attackers may use urgency, impersonation, authority, or curiosity.

Account security begins with strong authentication. Unique passwords, password managers, multi-factor authentication, and careful recovery settings reduce common account risks.

14. Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication uses two or more independent categories of evidence to verify identity. It can reduce the consequences of a compromised password.

15. What Is a VPN?

A virtual private network creates an encrypted connection between a device and a VPN endpoint. VPNs can improve protection on untrusted networks but do not provide complete anonymity.

16. Cookies Explained

Cookies are small pieces of browser-stored data used by websites for sessions, preferences, measurement, and other functions. Their security depends on how they are created and handled.

A data breach involves unauthorized access to or disclosure of information. Organizations use layered controls to reduce both the probability and impact of breaches.
